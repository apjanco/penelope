# The Woolf variance finding, assessed against modernist studies

![Left: Woolf's ten titles on the Cohn mediation cline, with the within-book spread implied by the reported eta-squared. Right: between-book variance share with all titles and with each author's most extreme title removed.]({{artifact:art_8bafc055-ba93-4a9f-b83a-5ec4014d8834}})

## The claim's actual load-bearing content

The η² = 0.53 figure is not a finding about ten novels; it is a finding about one. Removing *The Waves* and holding the within-book spread fixed drops Woolf's between-book share from 0.53 to 0.16, and the single title accounts for 76% of the between-book sum of squares. The other nine novels span 0.35–1.00 on a 0–3 scale — adjacent titles are separated by 0.00–0.40 within-book standard deviations, i.e. by nothing. Woolf's "high book-dependence" is the arithmetic shadow of a single outlier, and the same is true of the comparison: Joyce's 0.22 collapses to 0.02 without *Ulysses*, and Faulkner's 0.02 is computed from two titles, where η² cannot express a developmental pattern even in principle.

This matters because the note's headline sentence — that for Woolf "it depends enormously" which book you are reading, and for others it does not — is a claim about *dispersion across her career*, while the statistic supports only a claim about *one book's distance from the rest*. Those are different literary-historical propositions and they license different arguments. The second is defensible on this evidence; the first is not. The note half-recognizes this in its "shape of a career, not a style" section, but then keeps using the variance number as though it measured career-long variability.

A second arithmetic point cuts against the cross-author ranking. η² is not comparable across authors with different title counts and different within-book noise. Back-solving the implied within-book SD from each reported η² gives ~0.57 for Woolf and ~1.12 for Joyce and Faulkner — the Joyce/Faulkner texts are internally about twice as heterogeneous passage-to-passage. Feed Joyce's three book means through Woolf's within-book SD and his η² rises from 0.22 to 0.52; feed Woolf's ten means through Joyce's and hers falls from 0.53 to 0.23. The entire Woolf-vs-Joyce contrast is recoverable from a difference in *within*-book homogeneity, not from a difference in between-book range — whose raw SDs are in fact nearly identical (0.606 vs 0.596). Under the null of no real book differences, η² is also upward-biased with group count (E[η²] ≈ 0.03 at k=10, n=30 per book, vs 0.02 at k=2), so the author with the most titles is structurally advantaged on this metric. The honest statistic here is a variance-components model — a mixed-effects intercept for title, nested in author, with the passage-level residual estimated freely — reporting an intraclass correlation with an interval, not a point η² per author.

## What in the field this speaks to

The reception argument the note engages is real and current. Zhang's account of Woolf reads her descriptive technique as doing philosophical work that the Joyce-centred history of modernist innovation has undersold ([Zhang 2020](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226722665.001.0001), esp. the Woolf chapter, [Zhang 2020](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226722665.003.0005)); Attridge's recent work on Woolfian atmosphere and scale continues that line ([Attridge 2025](https://doi.org/10.1353/mfs.2025.a958564)). The taxonomy the measurement operationalizes is the standard one ([Cohn 1978](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691213125), extending [Cohn 1966](https://doi.org/10.2307/1770156)), and it has been revisited rather than superseded ([McHale 2012](https://doi.org/10.1353/nar.2012.0004)). So the finding is addressed to a live conversation with the right vocabulary, which is more than many computational forays into modernism manage.

But it lands in a conversation where the specific conclusions are already established by other means. That *The Waves* is generically discontinuous with Woolf's other fiction is close to consensus, and it has been argued precisely on the axis the note measures: Graham's reading of the novel's style shows it abandoning the narratorial positioning that organizes the other novels ([Graham 1970](https://doi.org/10.3138/utq.39.3.193)), and the "playpoem" framing treats it as a different genre rather than a more extreme novel ([McNichol 2018](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781351120500-7)). Woolf's own designation of it as an "abstract, eyeless" book is the standard citation. Sotirova's stylistic study of modernist consciousness presentation covers Woolf's free indirect style in detail and locates its historical transformations ([Sotirova 2013](https://doi.org/10.1057/9781137307255); [Sotirova 2007](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789401205313_008)). A specialist will read the ranking table and recognize their own syllabus ordering.

The quantitative half of the field has already done a version of this measurement. Brooke, Hammond and Hirst built a lexical-style model to quantify free indirect discourse in modernist fiction, tested on *To the Lighthouse* among others ([Brooke, Hammond and Hirst 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/llc/fqv072)); Brunner's earlier system automatically classifies speech, thought and writing representation in narrative ([Brunner 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/llc/fqt024)); and the Semino–Short corpus-stylistic scheme supplies the annotated ground truth that this tradition works against. Long and So's classification of modernist prose is the canonical demonstration that a stylistic distinction modernists cared about is machine-learnable ([Long and So 2016](https://doi.org/10.1086/684353)). The note's contribution relative to these is a *scale-position regression* rather than a category classifier, and a cross-author variance decomposition — genuinely not done in that form, as far as the retrieved literature shows.

## Where the novelty actually is

Three things here are new, and none of them is the 53%.

The first is the *ordinal placement of The Waves above Ulysses and both Faulkner novels* on a single graded mediation scale. Cross-author claims of this kind are normally made rhetorically; putting Woolf's most extreme book above Joyce's on a measured axis is a falsifiable version of a claim the reception history usually makes only in the reverse direction. It is also the finding most exposed to the objection that the scale is not doing what it says — a first-person present-tense soliloquy scores high on "no narratorial frame" almost by grammatical construction, while *Ulysses* spends most of its length in modes (Eumaeus, Ithaca, Cyclops) that are heavily mediated in ways orthogonal to Cohn's cline. The measurement may be reporting that *The Waves* is uniformly one thing and *Ulysses* is many things, which is not the same as Woolf out-radicalizing Joyce.

The second is the *contrast between kinds of low variance* — Faulkner consistently extreme versus James consistently restrained. This is the note's sharpest interpretive move and the one least available to close reading, because it requires holding two authors' whole outputs on a common scale simultaneously. It is also the point where the two-title Faulkner sample is weakest.

The third is the *negative result about Woolf's nine other novels*. Nine books across 1915–1941, spanning the invention of her mature method, land inside a 0.65-wide band on a 3-point scale. If that survives scrutiny it is a more interesting claim than the outlier: it says the technical revolution between *Night and Day* and *Mrs Dalloway* is not primarily a change in narratorial distance, which is roughly how the standard account describes it. The note buries this as a baseline.

## The critique to expect

**Operationalization.** Moretti's framing of measurement as operationalizing a concept ([Moretti 2014](https://doi.org/10.15794/jell.2014.60.1.001)) is the friendly version of the question a modernist will ask: does a passage-mean on a four-point ordinal scale measure "narratorial distance," or does averaging destroy the object? Cohn's categories describe *transitions* — the interest of free indirect discourse is the sentence-by-sentence oscillation between voices, the "dual voice" whose reality has itself been tested experimentally ([Bray 2007](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963947007072844)). A book mean of 0.87 for *Mrs Dalloway* is compatible with prose that alternates violently between 0 and 2 and with prose that sits flatly at 1, and the first is what criticism says Woolf is doing. Reporting only the mean discards the variable that the technique consists of. Within-book *dispersion* and autocorrelation of the score along the text are the quantities a narratologist would actually want, and they are already computed in this pipeline.

**Treating the scale as interval.** Averaging codes 0–3 assumes the psycho-narration→narrated-monologue step equals the quoted→autonomous step. Cohn does not claim that, and the note's own *To the Lighthouse* example — models unstable between categories 1 and 2 — is evidence that the intervals are not uniform. An ordinal model, or reporting the full distribution over categories per book, avoids the assumption.

**Validity of the annotation.** Two models agreeing is not inter-rater reliability in the sense the note invokes: two LLMs share training data, prompt framing, and failure modes, so agreement measures correlated bias as much as it measures signal. Krippendorff-style agreement statistics were designed for independent coders ([Krippendorff 2022](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781003112020-4)). The LLM-annotation literature supports LLM coding as *viable*, not as self-validating: the strong results are benchmarked against human gold labels ([Gilardi, Alizadeh and Kubli 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305016120)), and the systematic survey finds LLMs useful as zero-shot annotators while documenting where they fail and insisting on human validation ([Ziems et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00502)). For narrative-discourse annotation specifically, Piper and Bagga report qualified success and non-trivial disagreement with expert coders ([Piper and Bagga 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.wnu-1.4)). The fix is small: a few hundred passages double-coded by two trained human annotators against the same rubric, with agreement reported both human-human and human-model.

**Memorization.** This is the objection specific to canonical modernism and it is sharp. GPT-4-class models demonstrably know popular public-domain fiction well enough to reproduce passages ([Chang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.453)), and *Mrs Dalloway*, *To the Lighthouse*, *The Waves* and *Ulysses* are among the most-discussed novels in existence. A model asked how mediated a *Waves* passage is may be retrieving the critical consensus about *The Waves* rather than reading the passage. That the ranking reproduces the received syllabus ordering is, on this reading, a warning rather than a validation. The discriminating test is available: score passages from obscure contemporaries with the same technique, or hold-out-style test on unattributed, minimally-known texts, or check whether scores shift when titles and character names are masked. Masking is cheap and would answer the objection directly.

**Corpus composition.** *Flush* is a dog's biography and *Orlando* a mock-biography; including them lowers the nine-novel band and makes the "nine conventional novels" cluster partly an artifact of genre heterogeneity. Dropping both actually raises η² to 0.556, so the finding is not fragile in that direction, but a reader will want the choice defended rather than silent. Richardson's three volumes are installments of one work and are not independent observations of "book," which is what makes her <1% nearly tautological; the note says as much and should therefore not use her as a comparison case. James's three novels are unnamed in the note — if they are the late trio, their well-documented stylistic difference from his earlier work ([Hoover 2004](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963947004041970)) is being averaged away rather than found absent.

**Epistemology.** Da's critique is that computational literary studies frequently produce statistically weak results that restate what criticism already knows ([Da 2019](https://doi.org/10.1086/702594)); Bode argues that modelling choices carry interpretive commitments that cannot be corrected downstream ([Bode 2020](https://doi.org/10.1215/00267929-7933102)); Hankins raises the specific worry for digital modernist studies, whose canonical corpus is small enough that quantification adds little ([Hankins 2018](https://doi.org/10.1353/mod.2018.0040)). This finding is exposed on all three fronts, and the outlier-driven η² gives Da's version of the objection a concrete target. The constructive replies in the same debate are the model to imitate: Piper's argument for small-scale, hypothesis-specific modelling ([Piper 2017](https://doi.org/10.1632/pmla.2017.132.3.651)), So's insistence on stating what a model is and is not claiming ([So 2017](https://doi.org/10.1632/pmla.2017.132.3.668)), and Underwood's case that machine learning is valuable precisely where it models a *perspective* rather than a ground truth ([Underwood 2020](https://doi.org/10.1632/pmla.2020.135.1.92); [Underwood 2019](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226612973.001.0001)). Hammond's account of the validation double-bind in distant reading names the trap the two-model agreement claim falls into ([Hammond 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/lic3.12402)).

## What would make it publishable

The finding is relevant to the field, and the reception-history frame is the right one. As stated it would not survive review at *Modernism/modernity* or *Journal of Cultural Analytics*, for the reason a statistician and a modernist would give independently: the number reported is not the number that supports the argument. Five changes address that.

Report a mixed-effects variance decomposition with title nested in author and an interval on the intraclass correlation, and report the leave-one-title-out sensitivity alongside the headline value — an η² that moves from 0.53 to 0.16 on one deletion must be presented with that fact attached. Replace book means with within-book distributions and autocorrelation along the text, which measures oscillation rather than averaging it away, and which is where the narratological interest lies. Validate against human coders on a sampled subset and report human-model agreement, not model-model. Run the title/character masking test for memorization, and ideally add non-canonical contemporaries as a memorization control. Then reframe: the paper's claim is that *The Waves* is generically discontinuous with the rest of Woolf's fiction on a measured axis where nine other novels are indistinguishable, and that this discontinuity exceeds Joyce's and Faulkner's most extreme books — an argument about one book and one negative result, both of which the data can carry.

One further reframing is worth considering. The note's most defensible interpretive move is the one it makes last: that Woolf had the extreme register available and mostly declined it. That is a claim about *restraint as a choice*, and it does not require the variance statistic at all — it requires only *The Waves*'s position plus the flatness of the other nine. Zhang's argument would take that as friendly amendment rather than confirmation, and the finding is strong enough to make it.
