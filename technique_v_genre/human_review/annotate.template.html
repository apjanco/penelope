Rubric — the same definitions the machine readers were given

Rate how directly the passage renders consciousness against how much the narrator mediates it. This is an ordinal cline, not a set of categories: a passage sitting between two levels takes the one it leans toward, and your confidence should reflect how near the boundary it falls.

0 · psycho-narration
The narrator reports the mind from outside. She felt uneasy. He resented the intrusion. Analytic distance; the inner state is a fact the narrator states, not something rendered from within.
1 · narrated monologue
Free indirect discourse: the character's thought bends the narrator's own sentence, without quotation. Third person, past tense, but the idiom, rhythm and evaluative language belong to the character. Wasn't it odd that he never wrote?
2 · quoted monologue
Direct interior speech, explicitly framed. I am uneasy, she thought. Quotation marks or an equivalent tag mark the boundary between narration and thought, even though the thought is unmediated once inside the frame.
3 · autonomous monologue
Unframed, raw interior voice with no narratorial frame at all, sustained across the passage. Molly Bloom's soliloquy is the canonical case.

Two readers one level apart are in near-agreement — ordinary boundary tension, expected and informative. Two readers at 0 against 3 disagree about the basic nature of the passage.

Answer no to the first question only when the passage renders no consciousness at all — pure external description or action. A passage that is clearly interior but fits none of the named techniques is still consciousness: say yes, and choose Other.
